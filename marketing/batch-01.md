## 1. The faceless constraint — and the one call you should make

You picked product-only: no face, no narration. That's workable, but there's a
fork in the road worth deciding deliberately, because it changes what's possible.

The problem: LoopReady content is at its best when it shows a *conversation*.
A conversation needs two voices. The AI's voice is yours to use freely. The
candidate's voice is the question.

**Option A — AI voice out loud, candidate as on-screen text.** You hear the
interviewer speak; the candidate's answer appears as typed captions in a
distinct style. Zero founder voice.
- Pro: fully faceless *and* voiceless, cheapest, and it actually looks
  deliberate rather than like a compromise. It also foregrounds the AI's voice,
  which is the product's best asset.
- Con: slightly lower emotional intensity. You lose the sound of a human
  fumbling, which is a big part of why "watch someone get cooked" content works.

**Option B — AI voice out loud, your real voice as the candidate, no face.**
Standard faceless-creator territory: a voice is not an identity.
- Pro: unlocks the highest-performing formats (the dodge, the bomb-then-nail-it
  arc, self-cooking). Real human fumbling is the emotional payload.
- Con: it's your voice on the internet, permanently, attached to answers that
  are deliberately bad.

**My recommendation: A for the first two weeks, then B once something hits.**
Ship the whole first batch as pure Option A — it costs nothing to try and it
keeps your options open. If a post breaks out, the follow-up variants are much
stronger with a human voice in them, and by then you'll have evidence it's worth
it. Every script below is written for Option A with a note on what changes
under B.

**Your call, not mine.** Tell me if you'd rather start at B and I'll rewrite the
batch.

## 2. Content pillars

Four pillars for short-form, one for text platforms. Percentages are share of
the posting calendar.

### Pillar 1 — THE VERDICT (35%)
**The emotional job:** *someone finally tells me the truth about where I stand.*

The core of the product and the core of the brand. Every post in this pillar
cold-opens on real feedback output — the verdict, the "where you'd get dinged"
section, the reconciled loop signal. The product's honesty is the hook; you
never have to sell it, you just show it.

Formats: verdict cold-open, feedback-reveal scroll, the receipt (rubric on
screen), full-loop payoff.
Why it earns the biggest share: it's the only thing here that no competitor
demo can fake, and it's the emotional promise of the product stated in the
product's own words.

### Pillar 2 — THE INVISIBLE DING (25%)
**The emotional job:** *here is the specific thing nobody ever told you.*

Pure value, product as proof rather than pitch. Each post names one concrete,
unwritten behavior that reads as no-hire — not stating assumptions, dodging the
"tell me about a conflict" question, claiming a complexity you didn't verify,
narrating code instead of reasoning about it. The product appears as the
receipt: here's the app catching exactly that.

This is your **save-rate pillar.** These are the posts people bookmark before
their onsite. Saves are what train the algorithm that you're useful rather than
merely entertaining, and this pillar is also the one that works on LinkedIn.

### Pillar 3 — THE BAR (20%)
**The emotional job:** *am I actually at that level?*

Company and level calibration — the most defensible thing LoopReady does and
the thing nothing else in the category does at all. Same answer, two bars.
Same candidate, L3 vs L5. What "senior" actually means when a rubric applies it.

This pillar is where the product's differentiator is legible in a single shot,
and it's inherently argument-provoking — engineers will fight in the comments
about whether the bar is right, which is the strongest distribution signal
available.

### Pillar 4 — COOKED (15%)
**The emotional job:** *watching someone else get destroyed, safely.*

Entertainment. The interviewer refusing to accept a dodge, catching a wrong
complexity claim, quoting your own code back at you. Short, punchy, high share
rate, low save rate. Duet/stitch bait lives here.

Keep it at 15% — it's the most viral and the least convertive. It builds the
audience that the other three pillars convert.

### Pillar 5 — BUILT IN PUBLIC (LinkedIn / X only, not short-form)
What you learned building an AI interviewer, what calibration actually requires,
where the model gets it wrong. Text-first, no video. Reaches working engineers
and other builders. Product lives in the profile, not the post.

## 3. Platform focus & cadence

**Priority: YouTube Shorts ≈ TikTok > Reels.**

Shorts gets equal billing with TikTok here, which is not the usual advice — but
this audience *searches YouTube* for interview prep. A Short about the behavioral
round has a tail measured in months; a TikTok has one measured in days. Given
you're faceless (a format YouTube is friendlier to than TikTok is) and posting
at volume, the compounding search tail is worth as much as TikTok's cold reach.

Reels gets the same exports with no extra work. Don't build for it separately
until it shows you something.

**Cadence at 10+ hrs/week:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Mon | Shoot session 1 (~2h): 4–6 real product runs, raw capture |
| Tue–Wed | Edit into 4–5 posts (~3h) |
| Thu | Shoot session 2 (~1.5h): targeted captures for whatever the week suggested |
| Daily | 30 min: reply to every comment on anything <48h old |
| Daily | 30 min: genuine community participation, zero promotion |
| 2×/week | LinkedIn text post (Pillar 5) |

**Post 7–10×/week**, same cuts to all three platforms. Remove watermarks before
cross-posting — a watermarked TikTok uploaded to Reels gets suppressed.

Volume is the strategy at this stage. You have no data, so you have no idea
which hook family works. Ten posts teaches you more than one perfect post.

## 4. What "worked" means (the metric ladder)

Ignore views. Read the funnel stage by stage and name which stage broke:

| Stage | Metric | Early threshold | Failure means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 3s retention | >65% | First frame/line didn't earn attention |
| Hold | watch-through | >45% | Body didn't pay off the hook |
| Value | **saves** | >2% of views | Entertaining, not useful |
| Identity | **shares** | >1% of views | Didn't say something they wanted said for them |
| Intent | profile clicks | >1% of views | Content worked; CTA or profile didn't |

The two numbers that actually predict a channel working: **saves and shares.**
Everything else is diagnostic.

The most valuable single pattern to watch for: **high views, low saves.** That
means you have a winning hook attached to a weak body — reuse the hook verbatim,
replace everything after second 3. It's the cheapest win in short-form and the
first thing I'll look for when you report numbers.

**Report back per post:** views, 3s retention (or avg watch time), watch-through
%, saves, shares, comments, follows, profile clicks. Plus which hook variant you
used. I can work with partial data but the hook-variant label matters most.
